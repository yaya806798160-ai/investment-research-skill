# Investment Research Skill v1.0

## Purpose
A disciplined buy-side research workflow for public-market investing, with emphasis on growth equities, AI infrastructure, semiconductors, technology platforms, and thematic funds.

## Operating principle
Do not confuse a good business with a good investment. Separate:
1. Business quality
2. Industry opportunity
3. Cycle
4. Growth durability
5. Valuation
6. Market-implied expectations
7. Technical/entry conditions
8. Downside risk
9. Portfolio role

## Standard workflow

### Step 0 — Define the decision
Identify:
- security
- market
- investment horizon
- current position, if any
- intended action: buy / add / hold / reduce / exit / watch
- constraints such as liquidity, position size, QDII limits, or concentration

### Step 1 — Industry and value-chain map
Identify:
- upstream / midstream / downstream
- major suppliers and customers
- bottlenecks
- pricing power
- capacity and utilization
- capex intensity
- competitive structure
- geographic exposure

### Step 2 — Cycle diagnosis
Use Juglar-cycle logic where relevant:
- capex cycle
- inventory cycle
- utilization
- supply additions
- lead times
- pricing
- demand acceleration/deceleration

Classify the industry as early expansion, mid-cycle expansion, late expansion, slowdown, contraction, or recovery.

### Step 3 — Company quality
Assess:
- revenue growth
- gross/operating margin
- ROIC/ROE where meaningful
- free cash flow
- balance sheet
- dilution
- customer concentration
- competitive moat
- execution
- management capital allocation

### Step 4 — TAM and growth durability
Estimate:
- realistic TAM
- serviceable market
- plausible share
- revenue path
- margin path
- reinvestment requirements

Do not use headline TAM alone. Test whether the company can actually capture the market.

### Step 5 — Valuation
Use multiple cross-checks rather than one metric:
- P/E
- EV/EBITDA
- EV/Sales
- FCF yield
- PEG
- TAM-adjusted PEG
- scenario DCF where useful

Reverse the valuation: determine what growth, margin, or terminal assumptions are already embedded in the current price.

### Step 6 — Bayesian scenario analysis
Create bear/base/bull cases.
For each case define:
- assumptions
- probability
- revenue
- margin
- valuation multiple
- implied price/value
- key evidence that would increase/decrease probability

Update probabilities when new evidence arrives. Do not change probabilities merely because price moved.

### Step 7 — Technical health
Use 20/50/100/200-day moving averages when data is available.
Assess:
- trend alignment
- slope
- distance from moving averages
- volume confirmation
- support/resistance
- drawdown
- relative strength

Technical analysis determines timing and risk management, not business quality.

### Step 8 — Catalysts and risks
List:
- near-term catalysts
- medium-term catalysts
- structural risks
- valuation risks
- execution risks
- macro/geopolitical risks
- invalidation conditions

### Step 9 — Investment decision
Output one of:
- Buy
- Add gradually
- Hold
- Wait for better entry
- Reduce
- Exit
- Watchlist

The action must be tied to evidence, valuation, probability, risk, and timing.

### Step 10 — Position sizing
When requested, distinguish:
- thesis conviction
- valuation margin of safety
- volatility/drawdown
- portfolio concentration
- correlation
- liquidity

Never recommend position sizing solely from conviction.

## Data discipline
For current questions, use fresh and attributable data. Never fabricate:
- prices
- earnings
- analyst estimates
- valuation multiples
- technical levels
- market share
- catalysts
- financial figures

Label each important input as reported, estimated, assumed, or derived.

## Required final output
Every full research memo should contain:
1. Executive conclusion
2. Investment thesis
3. Industry/cycle
4. Company quality
5. Growth/TAM
6. Valuation and implied expectations
7. Bear/base/bull scenarios
8. Technical/entry assessment
9. Risks and invalidation conditions
10. Action and monitoring triggers
11. What would change the conclusion
